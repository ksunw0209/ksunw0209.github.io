I'm an AI researcher who wants to make fundamental breakthroughs with AI and use them to accelerate biomedical discovery. This fall I begin my PhD in Electrical Engineering & Computer Science at MIT, working at the intersection of machine learning and biology. I graduated from Seoul National University with a B.S. in Statistics.

Biology is where I think AI can matter most, but my deeper motivation is the breakthrough itself: building methods that change what is possible. I like to chase ideas by trying them directly — experiments, and wet-lab work when it helps — and letting the results steer where I go next.

Research

  • AI for life sciences

    Sharpening the hypothesis-generation ↔ experiment-design loop so that discovery moves faster. A central question I care about is modality transfer in biological data: how to carry information from modalities that are easy to scale but information-poor (e.g., cell imaging) to those that are hard to scale but information-rich (e.g., transcriptomics, genomics). I'm especially drawn to precision medicine and to molecular- and cellular-level modeling for cancer.

  • Diffusion model post-training

    Improving diffusion generative models after pre-training — through test-time scaling (spending more computation at generation time for better samples) and reinforcement-learning–based alignment and fine-tuning.

  • Biomolecular design & structure prediction

    Applying these methods to biology: designing proteins and antibodies toward a target function, and predicting protein structure — two ends of one pipeline from target to designed binder.
