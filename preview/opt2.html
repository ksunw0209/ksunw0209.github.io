I'm beginning my PhD in Electrical Engineering & Computer Science at MIT this fall, working on machine learning for biology. Before that, I studied statistics at Seoul National University.

I'm drawn to problems where a methodological idea changes which experiments are worth running. Most of my work so far has been on generative models — in particular, post-training diffusion models through test-time scaling and reinforcement learning — and applying those ideas to biomolecular design.

Going forward, I want to build models that make biomedical discovery faster: tightening the loop between forming a hypothesis and designing the experiment that tests it, and carrying information from biological data that is cheap but shallow into the richer, harder-to-scale measurements — with an eye toward precision medicine and cancer.
