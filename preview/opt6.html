I'm beginning my PhD in Electrical Engineering & Computer Science at MIT this fall, working on machine learning for biology. Before that, I studied statistics and mathematics at Seoul National University.

I'm drawn to problems where a methodological idea changes which experiments are worth running. Most of my work has been on generative models — in particular, post-training diffusion models through test-time scaling and reinforcement learning — which I'm now applying to protein structure prediction and biomolecular design.

Going forward, I want to build models that make biomedical discovery faster: tightening the loop between forming a hypothesis and designing the experiment that tests it, with an eye toward precision medicine and cancer.
